Understanding PRR

What is Patient Retention Rate (PRR)?

PRR measures the percentage of existing patients your healthcare organisation retains over a defined period. It's the single most undertracked growth metric in healthcare.

Formula
PRR = ((Patients at End − New Patients Acquired) ÷ Patients at Start) × 100

Real-world example

Start patients1,000
New patients acquired300
End patients1,100
PRR Result80%

(1,100 − 300) ÷ 1,000 × 100 = 80%

What 80% PRR means for revenue
200 of your original 1,000 patients did not return. At $400/visit × 3 visits/year, that's $240,000 in lost annual revenue — before factoring in the referrals those patients would have generated.
⚠ Even a "good" 80% PRR means 1 in 5 patients did not return.

FAQ

Common questions about patient retention rate

What is a good patient retention rate for a medical practice?
80% or above is generally considered strong for most medical practices. Specialist clinics and private hospitals often target 82–88%. Rates below 70% typically indicate systemic issues with patient experience, follow-up systems, or competitive positioning.
Why is patient retention more important than acquisition?
Retaining an existing patient typically costs 5–7× less than acquiring a new one. Retained patients generate higher lifetime value, refer at 3–4× the rate of new patients, and require less administrative friction per visit.
What causes patients to stop returning to a healthcare practice?
Common causes include long wait times, poor follow-up communication, lack of digital convenience (no online booking or portal), unaddressed negative reviews, and no systematic recall process. Many patients don't consciously leave — they simply aren't reminded to return.
How can digital marketing improve patient retention?
Through CRM automation (recall campaigns, appointment reminders), email re-engagement sequences, reputation management, and SEO strategies that keep your brand visible when existing patients search for related services. MedGrowth Engine specialises in all of these for healthcare organisations.
How often should a healthcare organisation calculate its PRR?
Most healthcare organisations should calculate PRR quarterly to identify seasonal trends and catch problems early. Annual measurement is the minimum. High-volume practices may benefit from monthly tracking integrated directly into their EMR or CRM dashboards.
